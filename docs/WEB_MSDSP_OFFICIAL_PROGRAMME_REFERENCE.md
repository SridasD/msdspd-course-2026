## 2. Programme Overview

The **MSc in Data Science and Product Development** is a two-year postgraduate programme that combines academic learning with supervised work on live digital projects.

The programme is intended to bridge the gap between university learning and professional product development by enabling students to:

- Learn advanced concepts in Artificial Intelligence, Data Science and software engineering.
- Apply knowledge through real project tasks, assignments and deliverables.
- Work within specialised Centres of Excellence.
- Follow professional development processes, quality standards and governance practices.
- Build an evidence-based professional portfolio.
- Receive performance-linked professional financial support, subject to eligibility and appraisal.

## 3. Work-Immersive Learning Model

The programme integrates three learning environments:

1. **Academic Learning** — expert-led theoretical and technical sessions.
2. **Applied Project Work** — supervised participation in live projects and product-development activities.
3. **Professional Development** — exposure to workplace tools, processes, reviews, standards and team practices.

Academic credits, assessments and learning outcomes are expected to be mapped to:

- Project tasks
- Assignments
- Deliverables
- Demonstrations
- Reviews
- Evidence of completed work

### Participating Centres of Excellence

- Centre for Digital Innovation and Product Development — **CDIPD**
- Applied AI Research Lab — **AIIRL**
- Centre for Geospatial Analytics — **CGA**
- Centre for Data Analytics — **CDA**

## 6. Semester-wise Learning Structure

### Semester I — Foundation and Ideation

**Published credit allocation:** 20 credits

**Academic direction:**

- Advanced AI and Machine Learning
- Full-Stack and cloud-development foundations
- Three elective skill or subject areas

**Project I:**

- Market research
- Problem and opportunity validation
- Technology-stack selection
- Initial solution architecture
- UI/UX prototyping

**Expected outcome:** A validated project idea, architecture blueprint and high-fidelity interface prototype.

### Semester II — Backend and AI / Full-Stack Engineering

**Published credit allocation:** 20 credits

**Core subjects:**

- CS103 — Modern Backend Systems and Data Engineering
- CS105 — DevOps and Automated Pipelines
- Three electives

**Project II:**

- Develop the AI model or full-stack solution components.
- Develop backend services and CRUD operations.
- Establish a locally executable working solution.

**Expected outcome:** A functional backend with a trained AI model, or a functional full-stack solution accessible in a local environment.

### Semester III — Integration and Cloud Orchestration

**Published credit allocation:** 20 credits

**Core subject:**

- CS104 — API Design and Microservices Orchestration
- Four electives

**Project III:**

- Integrate application layers.
- Connect approved third-party APIs and external data services.
- Integrate services such as payment gateways where relevant.
- Deploy the integrated solution to the cloud.

**Expected outcome:** A fully integrated and operational digital product.

### Semester IV — Solution Evaluation, Report and Viva

**Published credit allocation:** 20 credits

| Component | Credits |
|---|---:|
| Final project development within DUK/CDIPD Lab | 12 |
| Report and viva / final solution evaluation | 8 |
| **Total** | **20** |

**Expected outcome:** A professionally evaluated final product or solution, supported by a formal report, evidence portfolio and viva presentation.

## 11. Monitoring, Mentoring and Assessment

The published model includes continuous assessment by industry experts through:

- Sprint work-plan monitoring
- Activity-based monitoring using project-management tools
- Guidance by experienced project teams
- Monthly progress presentations
- Mentor evaluation
- Mapping of academic credits and outcomes to project evidence
- Performance-based appraisal for professional earnings

Students must be placed in a role involving **Artificial Intelligence** or **Full-Stack Development** as part of the mandatory professional track.

### Evidence Expected from the Learning Model

The official page does not prescribe a detailed evidence catalogue. Based on its stated work-immersive model, the portal should support traceable professional evidence such as:

- Source-code repositories and pull requests
- Technical documents and architecture records
- Data-analysis notebooks and model results
- UI/UX prototypes
- API specifications and test results
- Deployment records
- Project demonstrations
- Review comments and revisions
- Presentations and reflective reports

This evidence list is an implementation interpretation and requires academic approval before it becomes an official assessment rule.

## 14. Eligibility and Selection

### Eligibility

Applicants must have:

- A three-year or four-year Bachelor's degree in Science, Engineering or Mathematics.
- Mathematics or Statistics as one of the subjects.
- A minimum aggregate of 60% or equivalent.
- Applicable relaxation for reserved categories as per University norms.

### Entrance Routes

- CUET (PG)
- Digital University Admission Test — DUAT

**Published DUAT test code:** DUAT 07

**Applicable CUET (PG) codes:**

- SCQP09
- SCQP27
- SCQP19
- SCQP24
- MTQP04

### Selection Stages

1. Shortlisting through CUET (PG) or DUAT.
2. Machine-based Skill Assessment Test.
3. Interview and publication of the final selection list.

Reservation norms apply as per University rules.

## 15. Portal and Academic-System Implications

The official programme description implies that an academic activity portal should support the following information hierarchy:

```text
Programme
└── Semester
    ├── Core Subjects
    ├── Selected Electives
    ├── Project / Solution
    └── Sprint Work Plan
        └── Activity / Assignment
            ├── Expected Deliverables
            ├── Learning Evidence
            ├── Mentor Review
            ├── Revision
            └── Outcome Mapping
```

### Minimum Traceability Required

| Academic object | Must be traceable to |
|---|---|
| Subject or elective | Semester, credits and responsible faculty |
| Project | Semester outcome and participating Centre of Excellence |
| Activity | Subject, project, mentor and expected deliverable |
| Evidence | Student, activity, submission version and review status |
| Assessment | Rubric, reviewer, evidence and mapped PO/PSO |
| Sprint plan | Project activities, checkpoints and current status |
| Professional appraisal | Approved criteria and documented performance evidence |

### Important Boundary

The programme page refers to work-plan and activity monitoring. It does not state that portal login, time online or submission timestamps constitute attendance. Attendance rules and any DUK@360 integration must therefore be governed separately.
